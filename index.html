About

Why are we here?

These four books represent foundational books in the field of work and technology studies. Each of these books leverages qualitative methods to surface fundamental concepts which have gone on to influence the field of management and in particular theories around technology.

Our purpose in selecting these four books and accompanying papers was to provide an opportunity for scholars to be exposed to foundational texts around technology in their entirety or revisit these texts in the context of AI. By pairing these books with more current papers, we hope to foster a discussion that connects these books to current phenomena, providing modern context while reinforcing theoretical foundations.

This effort builds on the intellectual community around technology and long form scholarship started by Ruthanne Huising and Virginia Leavell in the 2025-2026 academic year with the History of Technology reading group. We look forward to exploring these books and papers together, as we continue this tradition.

How it works

Eight sessions, ninety minutes each, in four pairs. The first session of a pair focuses on the book. Three weeks later the second session focuses on three related papers, thirty minutes each. All meetings will be on Zoom.

Readings that come up in discussion are added here afterwards.

Reading the list

Each paper is marked Empirical or Theoretical. Titles link to the publisher's page and its abstract.

Everything on this page is in a Zotero group library, foldered by session. Anyone can browse it, and joining the group syncs it into your own Zotero. If you have trouble accessing a paper, email Daniel or Amanda.

Session 1 · Oct 14 & Nov 4, 2026

Book 1

JoAnne Yates — Control through Communication (1989)

Sloan Distinguished Professor of Management, Emerita, MIT Sloan · Google Scholar · MIT Sloan profile

Theme: How communication technology affected, and was affected by, record keeping and thus management practice. Based on historical archival data, this work lays the foundation for understanding how systems of producing, transmitting, and storing business information affects organizing and organizational structures.

Readings
  1. EmpiricalOrlikowski, W. J. & Yates, J. (1994). Genre Repertoire: The Structuring of Communicative Practices in Organizations. Administrative Science Quarterly, 39(4), 541–574.
  2. EmpiricalBailey, D. E., Leonardi, P. M. & Barley, S. R. (2012). The Lure of the Virtual. Organization Science, 23(5), 1485–1504.
  3. TheoreticalAlaimo, C. & Kallinikos, J. (2022). Organizations Decentered: Data Objects, Technology and Knowledge. Organization Science, 33(1), 19–37.

Session 2 · Jan 13 & Feb 3, 2027

Book 2

Shoshana Zuboff — In the Age of the Smart Machine (1988)

Charles Edward Wilson Professor Emerita, Harvard Business School · Semantic Scholar · HBS profile

Theme: A technology can automate work (do what people used to do), or it can informate it, turning the work into a stream of readable data. Zuboff's fieldwork in mills, offices and plants shows organizations reaching for the first and getting the second, and then having to decide who is allowed to read what, and who counts as skilled once the job is legible on a screen.

Readings
  1. EmpiricalAnthony, C. (2021). When Knowledge Work and Analytical Technologies Collide: The Practices and Consequences of Black Boxing Algorithmic Technologies. Administrative Science Quarterly, 66(4), 1173–1212.
  2. EmpiricalPachidi, S., Berends, H., Faraj, S. & Huysman, M. (2021). Make Way for the Algorithms: Symbolic Actions and Change in a Regime of Knowing. Organization Science, 32(1), 18–41.
  3. TheoreticalKellogg, K. C., Valentine, M. A. & Christin, A. (2020). Algorithms at Work: The New Contested Terrain of Control. Academy of Management Annals, 14(1), 366–410.

Session 3 · Apr 7 & Apr 21, 2027

Book 3

Lucy Suchman — Human–Machine Reconfigurations (2007)

Professor Emerita of the Anthropology of Science and Technology, Lancaster University · Google Scholar · Lancaster profile

Theme: Situated action. Anyone who has used a piece of digital technology has encountered a situation where their intent seems to be at odds with the technology's design. Suchman explores how the limitations of a machine's contextual knowledge challenges human-machine communication and the ability for people to achieve their intended action. Specifically, she shows how plans are resources people use to act, not scripts that determine what they do, by exploring machines built on the opposite assumption.

(Note: Please read the 2007 edition rather than the original Plans and Situated Actions so that we can all discuss the additional chapters.)

Readings
  1. EmpiricalBarrett, M., Oborn, E. & Orlikowski, W. J. (2012). Reconfiguring Boundary Relations: Robotic Innovations in Pharmacy Work. Organization Science, 23(5), 1448–1466.
  2. EmpiricalKim, B., Rezazade Mehrizi, M. H. & Bailey, D. E. (2025). Interlacing Situated and Algorithmic Modes of Knowledge Work: A Workplace Jurisdiction Perspective. Academy of Management Journal, 68(5), 907–938.
  3. TheoreticalAnthony, C., Bechky, B. A. & Fayard, A.-L. (2023). "Collaborating" with AI: Taking a System View to Explore the Future of Work. Organization Science, 34(5), 1672–1694.

Session 4 · Jun 30 & Jul 21, 2027

Book 4

Julian E. Orr — Talking about Machines: An Ethnography of a Modern Job (1996)

Formerly Xerox PARC · Semantic Scholar

Theme: Communities of practice. Xerox copier technicians diagnose problems their manuals cannot describe, and they do it by telling each other stories over breakfast, in the field, and in front of the customer. The knowledge lives in an occupational community rather than a repository, and so do the identity and the memory that come with it.

Readings
  1. EmpiricalBechky, B. A. (2003). Sharing Meaning Across Occupational Communities: The Transformation of Understanding on a Production Floor. Organization Science, 14(3), 312–330.
  2. TheoreticalBrown, J. S. & Duguid, P. (1991). Organizational Learning and Communities-of-Practice: Toward a Unified View of Working, Learning, and Innovation. Organization Science, 2(1), 40–57.
  3. TheoreticalChristin, A. (2020). The Ethnographer and the Algorithm: Beyond the Black Box. Theory and Society, 49, 897–918.
